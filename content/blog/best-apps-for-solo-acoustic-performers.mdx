A band splits the problems up. Somebody else holds the tablet, somebody else keeps time,
somebody else fills the space between chords with a second instrument. A solo performer is
holding the guitar, singing, and reading the words at once, with nobody free to turn a page —
which makes a few things matter more here than they do for a band, and a few things stop
mattering at all.

## When you need to fill the sound yourself

**OnSong.** The strongest case for a solo act that runs backing or click tracks: it plays them
alongside the sheet, sends MIDI to a looper or drum machine, and takes a wireless foot pedal so
your hands never leave the strings to turn a page. Free to build a library in; Essentials $29.99
a year, Premium $59.99 a year for the tracks and MIDI. iPhone, iPad and Mac only. [Full
comparison](/blog/onsong-alternative).

**JustChords.** The same idea from a different angle: audio tracks attached from Spotify or
Apple Music per song, AUv3 so the whole thing runs as a plugin inside GarageBand or Logic Pro if
you are building loops there, and a metronome and tuner already in the app you are reading from
— one less thing to dig for between songs. Free, with optional tips. Also iPhone, iPad and Mac
only. [Full comparison](/blog/justchords-alternative).

## When you want to own the app and stay cross-platform

**SongBook ChordPro.** No subscription, no account required to use what is already on the
device, and it runs on Android as well as Apple — the practical choice if a backing rig is not
the point and you would rather buy the app once. Bluetooth pedal support and a metronome cover
the two things a solo player usually needs the most: hands-free scrolling and a steady click.
[Full comparison](/blog/songbook-chordpro-alternative).

## When the room is the instrument you are missing

This is the one nobody else on this list does, and it is specific to playing alone: a solo
acoustic set often wants the audience singing, and a solo performer has no bandmate to pass
lyric sheets around.

**Strumfolio.** Strum Together turns that into a link. Share it before the song, and anyone in
the room — a stranger at an open mic, a table that just sat down, an audience of one at a house
concert — follows the same words in the same key on their own phone, with no account, no
install, nothing to hand out and collect afterward. It is the closest thing here to a second
voice on stage, and it costs the room nothing to use.

For the reading itself: a capo suggestion works out which fret leaves the most open shapes when
a song sits better a step or two from where it is written — useful the moment you are re-keying
something to fit your own voice rather than the original singer's. That one you can try on your
own chords right now, without an account: the [capo calculator](/tools/capo-calculator) runs
the same arithmetic, and the [transposer](/tools/chord-transposer) is the other half of it. The
free plan is a real repertoire with no end date, one songbook and thirty songs, which is enough
to hold an actual solo set list — and a
[setlist length calculator](/tools/setlist-length-calculator) for working out whether it fills
the slot. [How to bring an existing set of chord sheets in](/blog/convert-chord-sheets-to-chordpro)
covers moving a repertoire over without retyping it.

**Worst at:** everything the first two apps are built around. No foot pedal, no backing or
click tracks, no MIDI, no metronome or tuner. If the act depends on a loop pedal running from
the same app that shows the chords, look at OnSong or JustChords instead.

## Picking one

- **The set runs on backing tracks, a click, or a hardware looper synced by MIDI:** OnSong.
- **Same idea, but you would rather build it inside GarageBand than pay a yearly rig fee:**
  JustChords.
- **You want a metronome and a pedal, bought once, on whatever device you have:** SongBook.
- **The set is just you, your instrument, and you want the room reading along without printing
  anything:** Strumfolio.

None of these is wrong for a solo act — they are answers to different questions about what
"alone on stage" needs help with. If there is a band after all,
[the six-app round-up](/blog/best-chord-chart-apps-for-gigging-musicians) is the wider list, and
[the offline-specific one](/blog/best-offline-chord-chart-apps) covers what happens when the
venue has no signal either.
