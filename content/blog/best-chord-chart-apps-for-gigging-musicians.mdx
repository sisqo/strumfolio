"Best chord chart app" is a different question from "best guitar app." A gigging musician is
not learning a song, they are reading one they already know, on a stand, with a set to get
through and no time to fight the interface between songs. That narrows the field a lot faster
than a general top-ten does.

Six apps below, picked because each is a real answer for somebody, and none of them for
everybody. What each is worst at matters as much as what it is best at — that is the part a
sales page never says out loud.

## OnSong

The deepest stage rig of the six. It reads text charts and PDFs, transposes and capos both,
scrolls hands-free, takes a wireless foot pedal, plays backing and click tracks, sends MIDI,
and projects lyrics to a second screen. Free to download and build a library in; Essentials is
$29.99 a year for two devices, Premium $59.99 a year for four devices plus annotations, tracks,
MIDI and projection. Prices as listed in August 2026 — check the current ones before
deciding.

**Worst at:** existing anywhere but Apple. iPhone, iPad and Mac only — no Android, no web app
for reading from. Full comparison: [an OnSong alternative](/blog/onsong-alternative).

## SongBook ChordPro

The one built on the same open format Strumfolio reads and writes. iPhone, iPad, Android,
Windows and macOS — genuinely cross-platform, ahead of every Apple-only entry here before
anything else is said. It reads ChordPro and `.crd`/`.tab` files natively, auto-zooms and
auto-scrolls, ships chord libraries reaching past guitar into mandolin, banjo and piano, and
has a metronome and Bluetooth pedal support. Paid per platform, per store.

**Worst at:** a subscription-free plan that stays useful — you buy it once, but you buy it
again on the next platform. Full comparison: [a SongBook alternative](/blog/songbook-chordpro-alternative).

## JustChords

The most feature for the least money, if your band is on Apple. Transpose, capo, or rewrite a
song into a new key outright; a metronome and tuner in the same app; chord diagrams for
guitar, ukulele and piano that play the chord back to you; audio tracks attached from Spotify
or Apple Music; Bluetooth and MIDI pedals; AUv3, so it runs as a plugin inside Logic Pro and
GarageBand; PlayTogether, which keeps a whole band's setlists in sync live. Free, with optional
tips rather than a subscription.

**Worst at:** the same ceiling as OnSong. iPhone, iPad and Mac, nothing else, and PlayTogether
only syncs a band that is entirely on those. Full comparison:
[a JustChords alternative](/blog/justchords-alternative).

## Ultimate Guitar: Chords & Tabs

The one to open when the song is not yours. A crowd-sourced library in the millions, on iOS,
Android and the open web, useful the moment you need a chart for something you did not write
down yourself. Pro runs $29.99 a year with a 100-tab offline cap; Pro+ at $59.99 a year lifts
the cap and adds PDF export. Prices as listed and worth re-checking, as with every app here.

**Worst at** the two things a stage rig needs and a tab library does not promise: accuracy and
calm. A crowd-sourced chart is only as good as whoever transcribed it, and the free tier is
built around ads on a page you are trying to read at a glance — it is the app Strumfolio's own
design notes name directly as what a chord sheet should not look like.

## Songsterr

Built for tab accuracy first — synced playback, per-instrument mute, tempo control — with
chords as part of the package rather than the whole of it. Best for a bass or drum part you
need to nail exactly, less suited to a vocal-led set of lyrics and chords. Plus is $9.99 a
month and unlocks looping, pitch shift and offline playback of tabs you have already opened.

**Worst at:** being a lyrics app. There is no songwriting, no repertoire of your own words to
organize — it is a library to play along with, not a stand for your own set.

## Strumfolio

Where the browser fits into this list: the same address opens on an iPad, a Pixel, a Windows
laptop and whatever the drummer is holding, with no store and no per-platform purchase. Once a
song has been opened it stays readable with no signal. The free plan holds a real repertoire
with no end date — one songbook, thirty songs — and reads back in international, Italian,
German or Nashville-number notation, chosen per reader rather than per song. [How to convert
existing chord sheets into ChordPro](/blog/convert-chord-sheets-to-chordpro) covers moving a
library in without retyping it.

The one thing here that has no equivalent above: Strum Together shares a link, and anyone who
opens it — no account, no install, no purchase — follows the same song in the same key from
their own screen. A guest who arrived ten minutes ago can be reading along before the count-in.

**Worst at:** everything OnSong and JustChords are best at. No PDF import, no annotation, no
pedal, no MIDI, no backing or click tracks, no metronome or tuner. If the act runs on any of
those, it runs better on one of the other five.

## Picking one

- **The whole band is on Apple and the charts are PDFs:** OnSong.
- **The whole band is on Apple and money is the constraint:** JustChords.
- **The band wants to own the app outright, on whatever devices they already have:** SongBook.
- **The song is somebody else's and you need a chart fast:** Ultimate Guitar.
- **The set is instrumental-heavy and the tab has to be exact:** Songsterr.
- **The devices in the room do not match, and you would rather send a link than an install:**
  Strumfolio.
