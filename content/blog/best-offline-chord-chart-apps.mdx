"Works offline" gets printed on a lot of app store pages, and **it means at least three
different things.** Sometimes it means the whole library lives on the device from the start.
Sometimes it means a paid tier lets you download a fixed number of items in advance. And
sometimes it means whatever you already opened stays open — which is real, but only for what
you remembered to open before the signal dropped.

Those three are not degrees of the same promise. **The first keeps working on a device that
has never seen a network; the third keeps working only for songs you touched while you had
one.** Both are honestly described as offline, and the difference between them is the
difference between a set that happens and a set that does not.

A basement rehearsal room, a festival field, a venue that has never had reliable wifi: the
apps below, sorted by which of those three you are actually getting.

## Built offline-first

**SongBook ChordPro.** Reads its files off the device, full stop. No account is even required
to use what you already have on it — sync through Dropbox, OneDrive, Nextcloud or SongBook
Cloud is there if you want it, not a requirement to read a song. iPhone, iPad, Android, Windows
and macOS, bought per platform. [Full comparison](/blog/songbook-chordpro-alternative).

**OnSong.** The library sits on the device once it is in your account, and everything that
makes it a stage rig — hands-free scroll, the foot pedal, backing and click tracks, MIDI —
keeps working with no connection, because none of it was ever calling out to a server mid-set.
iPhone, iPad and Mac only. [Full comparison](/blog/onsong-alternative).

**JustChords.** Same story for the sheet itself — transposing, capo, diagrams, the metronome
and tuner all run locally. One real exception: the audio tracks feature streams from Spotify or
Apple Music, so *that* one feature needs a connection even though the chart it plays along with
does not. iPhone, iPad and Mac. [Full comparison](/blog/justchords-alternative).

## Offline as a paid add-on, with a cap

**Ultimate Guitar Pro.** The base app is a tab library that expects a connection. Pro adds
offline downloads capped at 100 tabs at a time; Pro+ removes the cap. Fine if you plan the set
list a day ahead and download it deliberately — not fine if you are relying on the free tier
and assuming "Pro" already covers you.

**Songsterr Plus.** Offline here means "previously opened tabs stay available," with cache size
set in the app's settings — the same shape of promise the browser-based apps below make, gated
behind a $9.99-a-month subscription rather than free. Prices as listed in August 2026.

## Cache-as-you-read, and what that actually promises

**Strumfolio.** No install and no per-platform purchase — the same URL is the app everywhere —
and once a song has been opened, it is cached and stays readable with no signal, indefinitely,
on the free plan and every plan above it.

The honest caveat, stated plainly rather than left for a reader to discover on stage: this is
**cache-as-you-read**, not a bulk "download my songbook" button. A song that has never been
opened is not on the device, and no signal means no first look at it. The fix costs nothing and
takes thirty seconds — open every song in tonight's set once, while there is still a signal to
open it with, ideally the night before rather than in the car park. [How ChordPro sheets get
into Strumfolio in the first place](/blog/convert-chord-sheets-to-chordpro) if the set is not
in the app yet, and the [ChordPro converter](/tools/chordpro-converter) if you would rather
try the conversion before signing up for anything.

## What does not make this list

A tab site that only exists as ads-supported pages in a browser tab is not on it, however good
the chords are, because closing the tab or losing signal loses the page. That is a different
failure from anything above: it is not a cap or a feature gate, it is the absence of an offline
mode at all.

## Picking one

- **You want to buy an app once and never touch a settings menu for it:** SongBook.
- **The set needs a foot pedal, backing tracks or MIDI, with or without signal:** OnSong.
- **You are already paying Ultimate Guitar or Songsterr and just need to remember to download:**
  do that a day ahead, not at soundcheck.
- **The room has mixed devices and you would rather open every song once tonight than manage a
  download list:** Strumfolio.

## The five-minute check before you leave the house

**Whichever one you pick, the habit matters more than the app.** None of these can keep a
promise about a song nobody ever opened, so the routine is the same in all of them:

1. **Open tonight's set, song by song, on the device you are taking.** Not the list — the
   songs. Opening a setlist is not the same as opening what is in it.
2. **Do it on the device that goes to the gig**, not on the laptop where you edited the
   sheets. Caches are per device, and so are downloads.
3. **Then turn the wifi off and look again.** Thirty seconds, at home, where being wrong is
   free. This is the only step that actually proves anything.
4. **Charge the thing.** An offline chart on a dead tablet is a printed setlist you did not
   bring.

Step three is the one people skip and the one that catches everything: a wrong assumption
about what "offline" meant in your app is very cheap to discover in the kitchen and very
expensive to discover in the room.
