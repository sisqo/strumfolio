## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment: unmistakably authored for Strumfolio.** The reading mechanics could not be lifted into another product. The Key chip's value badge *is* the way back to the written key. The Capo menu draws one dot per chord per fret — a density map of how many chords go open at each position — and offers "At fret 5, 5 of 6 chords are open" with a one-tap Move. The Chords menu renders four live previews built from *this song's own chords, transposed and spelled as the sheet currently shows them*; the option previews itself. `useAutoScroll` suspends rather than stops, settles on 400ms of page stillness rather than a fixed delay because a phone's momentum glide outlasts the finger, and accumulates fractional pixels so the slowest of eight speeds doesn't round to zero. Those are bugs only found on stage.

Where the authored voice weakens is **the frame around the sheet, not the sheet**. At 1440 the dock centres at x=463, the song card at x=548, and the prev/next capsule floats at x=1032–1190 — three objects, three centres, 356px of empty rail between the two controls a player touches most. The phone is composed; the desktop is arranged.

**Deterministic scan.** `detect.mjs` over the reader's transitive component set: **exit 2, 4 findings, all `design-system-font-size`, all advisory** (`LiveSong.tsx:110` `1.6875rem`; `UserMenu.tsx:364`; `SongRow.tsx:63,84`). Triaged: `1.6875rem` is off DESIGN.md's ramp but appears in exactly two places, both the song title, character-identical — deliberate and consistent, just undocumented. The other three appear 28, 53 and 19 times in `globals.css`: de-facto ramp steps DESIGN.md never wrote down. **The finding is that the documented ramp is incomplete, not that the code is inconsistent.**

A correction to what this run was told at the outset: the DEGRADED banner does **not** mean contrast went unevaluated on this target. `HTML_EXTENSIONS` is `.html`/`.htm` only (`detector/node/file-system.mjs:32`), so `.tsx` and `.css` always route to the regex engine regardless — there was nothing to un-degrade on a source scan. The parser modules were installed into a scratch tree anyway and the non-degraded engine was pointed at **rendered HTML with the stylesheets inlined**, which is where it earns its keep: 8–12 DOM-matched findings per view, and it resolved `var(--)` chains to compute real contrast.

**Visual overlays: none.** The browser ran headless with no user-visible tab, so **no `[Human]` overlay tab exists**. `detect.js` was genuinely injected and executed in-page — that evidence is console/API only: light-phone and light-desktop resting returned 0 findings; dark-phone returned 4; dark-phone with the capo menu open returned 21, of which **16 text-occlusion hits are false positives** (the coverer is the opaque `.chip-menu` itself). Also false: `Outfit Fallback`/`Geist Mono Fallback` (next/font metric-fallback face names, both faces declared), `__nextjs-Geist` (dev overlay), `overused-font: geist mono` (wrong for this app — `layout.tsx:18` confines it to ChordPro), the monogram avatar colour (a *declared* exception at DESIGN.md:229), and `gpt-thin-border-wide-shadow` on the dock and panels, which is `--shadow-float`/`--shadow-panel` specified verbatim at DESIGN.md:276-277.

## Overall Impression

This surface is the product working. The chips row is a genuine design *fix* rather than a rearrangement, and the auto-scroll hook is built for the physical scene instead of the demo. Nothing here loses data or stops a player — there is no P0.

**The single biggest opportunity is that dark mode is where the design system stops enforcing its own rules**, and dark is the theme the stage scene exists for. Both assessments landed there independently and by different routes: every paired detector view returns more findings in dark than light (light-phone resting: 0; dark-phone resting: 4), the accidentals toggle's selected state computes to ≈1.05:1 in dark because it was given only the light theme's elevation mechanism, the green Move pill becomes the brightest, most saturated element on the entire screen — out-massing the amber chords on the one screen the Chord-First Rule was written to protect — and the play button carries an accent glow that DESIGN.md:169 rules out by name. The light theme is materially cleaner in every single paired view.

## What's Working

- **The chips row replaced two elements with one and got more capable.** `SongControls.tsx:35-51` states the reasoning: four controls that *state* something as well as set something can't live behind a button. They moved onto the sheet, and the separate "in words" line under the title — which existed only to repeat what the shut panel was hiding — was deleted with them. The value became the control.
- **`useAutoScroll` is designed for the scene, not the screenshot.** Suspend rather than stop so `running` never flickers and the wake lock is never dropped mid-song; touches on `.control-bar`/`.top-bar` excluded so tapping speed doesn't suspend the thing it's speeding up; resume gated on the page being still rather than a timer.
- **Both themes really are hand-tuned, and it was verified rather than assumed.** The Veil-Inversion Rule holds in practice — the dark veil lightens the page behind an open menu instead of darkening it. `--shadow-1` collapses to `none` in dark and `--edge` appears, exactly as documented. Chord amber `#f0b268` on `#181b21` is legible without being neon.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The controls a player taps mid-song are 30×30, and DESIGN.md contradicts itself about whether that's allowed.**
Why it matters: this is one-handed, eyes half-up, with a plectrum between two fingers. Both assessments measured the same targets independently at 390×844: `.song-chip-step` (lower/raise a semitone) and `.song-chip-toggle` (♯/♭) at **30×30**, `.song-chip-value` at 58.9×**30**, `.comments-segment` at **38×38**, `.capo-suggestion-action` at 58.2×**32**, `.speed-range` at 22px thin, `.fret-button` at 39.7 wide. A missed tap on a stepper is a wrong key, mid-song. DESIGN.md:406 commits to a 44px minimum "anywhere a control is tapped repeatedly mid-performance (control bar, **segmented buttons**)" — the ♯/♭ pair is exactly a segmented control — while DESIGN.md:326 documents the chips at 2.25rem/36px. The code is under *both* numbers.
Fix: resolve which rule governs the chips, then give the sub-elements a 44px hit area via padding or a `::before` overlay — the visual pill can stay 30px. The chips already wrap to two rows on a phone, so the height is affordable. Primary dock controls already pass (`.control-play` 46×46 desktop / 78×78 phone).
Suggested command: `adapt`

**[P1] `--faint` is used for real text in seven places, against the prohibition written directly above its own definition.**
Why it matters: `globals.css:60-63` states `--faint` is "Reserved for decorative glyphs … **never for real text**. It measures under 4.5:1 against every surface above, so any label, count, or placeholder a sighted reader is meant to read uses `--muted`." It is then given to `.comments-rail-empty` (measured **3.1:1** light, **3.0:1** dark), `.control-nav-count` (the "2/2" pill that is permanently on the reading screen, 13.5px), `.song-chips-note`, `.comments-rail-meta`, `.comments-rail-private`, `.sheet-orphans-label` and `.control-strum`. All are ≤13.5px, so the 4.5:1 body threshold applies to every one. `UserMenu.tsx:364` carries a comment applying the rule correctly, so the rule is known — these instances were missed. PRODUCT.md commits to "contrast checked across both hand-tuned themes rather than assumed from one"; only `.comments-rail-empty` was *caught* automatically because it is the one visible in a database-less session.
Fix: switch all seven to `--muted`. Mechanical, and it is the highest-confidence finding in the run — caught by two engines, in both themes.
Suggested command: `audit`

**[P1] Turning zoom up gives the reader less song, which is the inverse of what zoom is for.**
Why it matters: measured mid-song at 390×844, the reading window between the bars is 635px (75%) and holds **3 of 11 sheet lines at max zoom** (7 at the 20px default). At the top of a song — every open, every next-song step — 239px of title, actions row and chips drops that window to 396px (47%): 6 lines at default, **2 at max zoom**. Zoom exists so a musician can read a propped tablet at arm's length; the reader who most needs it gets the least song, because the chrome that scales *up* with zoom is competing with chrome that never scales down and never collapses on scroll.
Fix: collapse the heading block on scroll-down past ~200px — title to a single sticky line, actions row hidden, chips retained as a compact strip. The chips must survive; their whole argument is that a value worth reading can't be hidden. The title, artist, section and five-button actions row need not.
Suggested command: `layout`

**[P2] Dark mode is where the system's own rules stop being enforced.**
Why it matters: three separate violations converge on the theme the stage scene exists for. (a) `.song-chip-toggle.is-on` computes to `#181b21` on a `#1d2027` track — **≈1.05:1** — with `box-shadow: none`, because `globals.css:1465-1469` gives this component only the light theme's elevation mechanism and `--shadow-1` is `none` in dark. "Am I in sharps or flats?" stays answerable but stops being glanceable. (b) The `#6fd88f` Move pill in the capo menu is the brightest, most saturated element on the screen — DESIGN.md's own defence of the favourite star is that it "is a single glyph and never an area — so it cannot mass into something a reader's eye lands on before the sheet." The Move pill is precisely an area, in a non-accent hue. (c) `.control-play` carries `box-shadow: 0 2px 6px color-mix(in srgb, var(--accent) 22%, transparent)` (`globals.css:11126`) — a coloured glow on the most prominent control, where DESIGN.md:169 says depth comes from the surface ladder "not from gradients, glow, or noise."
Fix: add `border: 1px solid var(--edge)` to `.song-chip-toggle.is-on` (or move its on-fill to `--surface-2`); drop the Move pill to a `success-soft` fill with `success` text; remove the accent glow from `.control-play` in dark.
Suggested command: `polish`

**[P2] DESIGN.md is stale about the app's most important screen.**
Why it matters: CLAUDE.md declares DESIGN.md the living token source kept in sync by hand. Three divergences on this surface. `--note` (`#2f5f8f` / `#8fbde4`, `globals.css:117-118`, `229-230`) drives ~20 rules here and is absent from the frontmatter, while the prose asserts `/accounts` "is the one place in the app with a second color family" and "Nowhere else in the app gains a second color family from this." `--success` fills the capo band and Move pill on the same footing. And the type ramp omits `0.9375rem`, `0.8125rem` and `0.78125rem`, which are used 28, 53 and 19 times respectively — they are ramp steps in practice. Separately, the 36px-vs-44px conflict under the first P1 lives here too. **None of this says the colours or sizes are wrong** — blue-as-annotation-ink is defensible and the green carries an argued comment at `globals.css:10743-10752`. The defect is documentation truth.
Suggested command: `document`

## Persona Red Flags

**The dark-stage player** (PRODUCT.md's own scene: "light ranges from a lit rehearsal room to a dark stage"). Cannot tell at a glance whether they are reading sharps or flats — `.song-chip-toggle.is-on` resolves to ≈1.05:1 against its track, with the meaning surviving only in the glyph's text colour. Opens the capo menu to find an easier fingering and the brightest object in their field of view becomes a green pill, on the one screen the Chord-First Rule was written to protect.

**The near-sighted reader on a propped tablet.** Sets text to the 30px maximum and gets 3 of 11 lines mid-song, 2 at the top of every song, because the 239px heading block and the 145px dock never shrink. Also hit by the 30×30 steppers and the 16px inline chord targets — and PRODUCT.md leads with "every chord is also a button."

**The one-handed guitarist mid-song.** Every control they reach for between verses is under the app's own floor: `.song-chip-step` 30×30, `.song-chip-toggle` 30×30, `.capo-suggestion-action` 32px tall, inline chords 16–17px wide. Compounding it, `useAutoScroll` deliberately routes space and the arrow keys to the *controls*, so there is no hands-free play/pause on a laptop either.

**PRODUCT.md's solo player stepping a set list.** On a propped tablet, Previous and Next sit at x=1032–1190 while play is at x=313 — the two things they touch most are at opposite ends of the screen with an empty notes card between them. And when the song ends, the loudest object on screen is a solid near-black **Delete** pill: DESIGN.md reserves that ink solid for "the confirmed step of a delete," but here it is the *unarmed trigger* while the actual confirm downgrades to a soft tint (`DeleteSongLink.tsx:56-77`). The treatment is inverted, and it lands on the peak-end moment.

**PRODUCT.md's band member annotating a part.** Meets three near-identical speech-bubble glyphs with no words on a phone (`.comments-segment-label` is `display: none` below desktop), a badge reading `0` in a hue used nowhere else on the screen, and an empty state instructing them to "Arm the pen." Nothing on that row says what a note *is*, or that only they will see it — that sentence lives in the rail, which does not exist on a phone.

## Minor Observations

- **At 1440 the reading bar is three objects at three centres** (P3): `.control-strip` centres on the whole `reading-layout` (x=720) while the words centre elsewhere, and the notes rail spends 328×163px saying "Nothing noted yet." Centring the strip on the song column and letting the rail collapse to a spine when empty would fix both at once.
- **Play is 78px on a phone and 46px on desktop**, the same size as its four siblings. DESIGN.md's "largest thing in the app on purpose — a destination rather than a nudge" is written about the phone dock; the distinction is simply dropped at 1440.
- **The favourite star ships as an area.** DESIGN.md's declared exception insists it is "a single glyph and never an area"; once lit it sits on a ~44px accent-soft chip.
- The `›` pager revealing alternate chord voicings is faint and half outside the card.
- No `scroll-margin-top` on sheet content — scroll-into-view parks elements under the 64px sticky top bar. Relevant to the `#song-<slug>` fragment the way-back link relies on.
- On desktop, auto-scroll reaches the bottom in ~10s and stops silently; the play button just flips back.
- Verified clean and worth recording: **no horizontal overflow at any viewport or state** (`scrollWidth === clientWidth` across 16 measured combinations), and the bottom Edit/Delete actions are genuinely reachable at max scroll (both 44×44+, `elementFromPoint` returns the button) — an early report that the dock covered them was retracted on measurement.
- Empty states are good: the notes rail teaches the interface rather than saying "nothing here," and `Previous` renders inert-and-unnamed rather than disabled-and-announced.

## Questions to Consider

1. **Should nudging the page back hold it?** `SETTLE_MS = 400` was chosen so a reader who nudged one line doesn't have to wonder whether they stopped the song — but the cost is that re-reading a phrase is impossible without finding pause, one-handed, with a guitar in the other. The design solved one half of the anxiety and created the other. Would a longer settle, or a visibly suspended play button, cost anything?
2. **Should the chips scroll away with the song?** Their argument is that a value worth reading can't be hidden — but they are read *before* the first chord and almost never after.
3. **Why is the capo's suggestion louder than the capo's actual state?** Fill-for-suggested and ring-for-current is an argued choice, but on stage "where is my capo" is asked far more often than "where could it be."
4. **Is Delete the right way to end a song?** Inverting the ink solid onto the confirm step would cost nothing and remove the loudest object from the peak-end moment.
5. **At 1440, why does a four-option menu open 500px from the chip that opened it?** The full-width-off-the-row rule was learned on a 390px phone; at desktop width it produces exactly the disconnect it was invented to prevent.
